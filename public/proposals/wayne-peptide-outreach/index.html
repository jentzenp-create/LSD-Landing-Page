The Gap

Why most outbound programs stall

The failure point is rarely the offer. It is almost always infrastructure, ownership, and measurement. Three problems that compound quietly until the whole program looks like it simply doesn't work.

Messages that never reach the inbox

Sending cold volume from an unprepared domain drives mail to spam and damages sender reputation. Reply rates collapse, and it looks like the offer failed when the message was never actually read.

Renting the growth engine

When domains, mailboxes, lead data, and workflows live inside a vendor's platform, leaving means rebuilding from zero. The pipeline is only as durable as the contract behind it.

No attribution, no learning

Without tracked lead status and clear sourcing, there is no way to tell which segment, message, or list produced a real account, so every round of spend repeats the same guesses.

The Strategy

Email-led, multichannel, human-closed

Email does the volume work: it starts conversations across hundreds of practices at a cost per touch nothing else matches. Calling does the qualification work on accounts worth the time. And you, or your sales team, do the closing work on the accounts that actually move the revenue number.

The system's job is to manufacture qualified conversations and route them cleanly to a human. It is not designed to replace the relationship-driven sale, because for a clinic committing to a new supplier, the relationship is the sale.

This proposal builds the first link in that chain: the cold email engine and the infrastructure underneath it. The CRM pipeline, post-call automation, and everything that follows are listed as optional add-ons further down, so you can add them once the outreach is proving itself rather than paying for them up front.

The Deliverability Advantage

The part most outbound skips

Deliverability is the difference between an outreach program and a spam folder. It is not a setting to switch on. It is a set of practices maintained continuously, and it is the central discipline of this build.

Built into the foundation
  • Sending domains kept separate from the main business domain
  • Proper domain and mailbox configuration
  • Email authentication configured correctly
  • Mailbox warm-up before meaningful volume
  • Controlled sending-volume ramp per mailbox
Maintained over time
  • Sender reputation and inbox health monitoring
  • Bounce and response monitoring with list hygiene
  • Volume adjusted to what the data supports
  • Copy and cadence tuned to reduce negative signals
  • Infrastructure expanded as demand justifies it

No provider can guarantee inbox placement, because mailbox providers make that call. What this build does is follow the practices that give a sending program its best structural chance, and catch problems early enough to correct them.

Rollout Plan

Validate → Scale → Automate selectively

Each phase unlocks the next. Nothing scales until the data says it should.

Phase 1 · Validate
Learn from the first 100

Run the existing ~100-lead list as a controlled test: personalized cold email from the new infrastructure, with your call follow-up on anyone who engages. The goal is answers: which buyer types engage, what objections surface, which value proposition resonates, and whether the lead quality justifies its cost.

Phase 2 · Scale
Add volume without breaking placement

Once messaging and conversion signals hold up, layer in larger, lower-cost lead sources alongside curated contacts for priority accounts. Sending volume increases gradually, within deliverability limits, and inbound replies get routed to you first.

Phase 3 · Automate
Selective, once the process is proven

AI calling and workflow automation come in only after there is enough volume and a tested sales process to justify them, supporting research, enrichment, personalization, reply triage, and scheduling. For large accounts, automation creates and qualifies opportunities; it does not replace the relationship.

Domain pricing is a standard-TLD estimate.

What that gap actually buys. It is not proof that Sellthium overcharges for what it does. A subscription like theirs bundles lead sourcing, sending, and support into one predictable monthly bill with nothing to configure. The cost above is only the wholesale price of the parts: domains, mailboxes, and sequencing software still need to be set up correctly, connected to each other, authenticated, warmed up, and monitored, which is exactly the work priced into this build's setup fee and ongoing management. Raw infrastructure is cheap. Running it correctly is the actual product.

On lead sourcing cost. Actor-based scraping tools can source raw contact records for well under a cent apiece before verification, a small fraction of Sellthium's $0.40 to $0.67 per lead. That is not a fully apples-to-apples number on its own: raw scraped records still need deduplication, validation, and segmentation before they are usable, which is the work already priced into this build's list preparation block. Once that work is counted, sourcing through this system still lands well under Sellthium's bundled rate for equivalent volume.

A practical note. These are not strictly either/or. At $99.95 to $449.95 a month, Sellthium is a plausible supplemental lead source to test in Phase 2 once messaging is validated, feeding records into infrastructure you own rather than replacing it. That call should come down to measured activation rate, not list price.
